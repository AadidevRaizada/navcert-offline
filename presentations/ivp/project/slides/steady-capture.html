TIME: 45 seconds. Unit 8. SAY: Navigators used dead reckoning: estimate where you are from how far you have moved. Video codecs do the same with pixels. SAY: The camera preview is a video. Consecutive frames are nearly identical, so for each block in the new frame we search the old one for the best match, the minimum sum of absolute differences. SAY: Exhaustive search over plus or minus 7 pixels tests 225 positions per block. Three-step search tests 9, then 8, then 8, which is 25, about nine times fewer. 2-D log search walks a plus-shaped pattern and averaged 20.7 points on my synthetic frames. In the app's Lab on the sample certificate the counts are 212.9, 24.4 and 20.0 points per block, and it detects a hand shake of minus 4, plus 3 pixels. SAY: In the Lab this is a steady-capture demo: if the motion vectors are near zero, the hand is still and the frame is safe to capture. In the app today, capture is a single still photo, so this is the Lab, not the main path. SAY: Those motion vectors plus a DCT-coded residual are exactly what P and B frames carry. CUE: the animation fills 225 cells, then three-step, then 2-D log, then compares the counts. The counts are produced by the animation's own search code. BACK-POCKET: Honest trade-off from my measurement (10 synthetic frame pairs, 480 by 640, 16 px blocks, noise sigma 2): on textured blocks exhaustive search found the exact vector 99.9 percent of the time, three-step 29.6 percent, 2-D log 32.8 percent. Cheaper searches can miss the true minimum on a noisy, non-monotonic error surface; exhaustive never does. Low-pass filtering the frames first raises them to 49.1 and 58.7 percent. BACK-POCKET: I, P and B frames: I is intra-coded and decodes alone; P is predicted from a past frame with motion vectors plus a DCT residual; B is predicted from past and future frames and is usually the smallest. BACK-POCKET: Complexity: (2p+1)^2 positions for exhaustive, each costing N squared absolute differences for an N by N block.

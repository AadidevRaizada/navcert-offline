TIME: 50 seconds. Units 4, 6, 7. SAY: Black-and-white mode needs a threshold. A global one fails: there is no value that keeps faint ink in the dim corner and clean paper in the bright one. SAY: Otsu is the textbook answer, the threshold that maximises between-class variance. It is optimal when the histogram has two clean peaks. A light gradient smears the paper into a wide band, and Otsu cannot separate ink from the dark side. SAY: So the app compares every pixel with the Gaussian mean of its own 35 by 35 neighbourhood, minus 11. That is adaptive thresholding. SAY: On the binary page, the Lab labels connected components, 4-connectivity breaks diagonal strokes, 8 keeps characters whole, and runs opening, closing and boundary extraction. SAY: Colour: in HSV, hue separates the red stamp from black ink whatever the lighting. That is colour slicing, Unit 7. CUE: the animation sweeps a global threshold, drops the Otsu value computed from the histogram, then shows adaptive. The Otsu value in the animation is computed live from this page. BACK-POCKET: Why does Otsu fail under uneven lighting? A shadow shifts the background level, so no single T keeps both regions. BACK-POCKET: Stamp detection: HSV, hue band plus minimum saturation; red wraps around hue 0 so use two bands. BACK-POCKET: m-connectivity uses diagonals only when the shared 4-neighbours are not foreground, removing 8-connectivity's double paths.

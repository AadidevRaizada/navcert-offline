TIME: 45 seconds. Unit 2 point and histogram processing, Unit 1 quantisation. SAY: Next, the light. Global histogram equalisation uses one dimmer switch for the whole page. The white paper dominates the histogram, so the shadow stays dark and flat paper turns grainy. SAY: CLAHE uses 64 dimmer switches, an 8 by 8 grid of tiles, each with its own mapping. Each tile's histogram is clipped at 2.2 times the average bin, which caps how much noise it can amplify. Tiles are blended bilinearly so there are no seams. SAY: The phone on the right is the in-app Pipeline Lab on the sample certificate: input, global equalisation and CLAHE side by side, with the histograms and entropy underneath. It also runs the textbook point operations: negative, contrast stretch, log, gamma and slicing, plus resolution and gray-level quantisation. CUE: the animation runs original, global equalisation, the tile grid, then CLAHE. The histogram and the clipped tile histogram are computed live from a generated page, not drawn by hand. BACK-POCKET: Why CLAHE and not global HE? Global HE builds one mapping from the whole-page histogram; CLAHE builds 64 clipped mappings, so a shadow in one corner does not wreck the rest. BACK-POCKET: The app's actual constants: clip 2.2, tiles 8 by 8, then a 3 by 3 median. BACK-POCKET: Rectification's gain comes mainly from restoring line order, not from better glyph recognition: word recall barely moves (92.9 percent raw versus 93.6 percent enhanced) while mean CER falls from 9.6 to 4.2 percent. Enhancement is the smaller, conditional step.

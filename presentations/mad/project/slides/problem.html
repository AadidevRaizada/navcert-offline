02

[45 s] Every seafarer and every ship runs on certificates: competency, medical fitness, safety, insurance. They must be valid, and someone must be able to read the dates. In practice these documents travel as paper originals, scanned PDFs and photos taken on a mess-room table, at an angle, in poor light. They are skewed, scattered across phones and email, and silent: you cannot search them and nothing tells you one is about to expire. The Tokyo MOU port state control regime recorded crew-certificate deficiencies rising from 877 in 2022 to 1,304 in 2024. A generic scanner fixes the picture but not the meaning. That is the gap I picked.

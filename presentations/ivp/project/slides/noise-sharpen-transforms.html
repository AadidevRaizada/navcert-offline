TIME: 45 seconds. Unit 2 neighbourhood processing, Unit 3 transforms. SAY: Here is a paradox: to sharpen, we blur. Subtract a Gaussian-blurred copy from the image and what is left is the edge detail. Add a fraction of it back, 32 percent in Auto, 50 percent in Clean. SAY: Before that, a 3 by 3 median removes the speckle that CLAHE lifted. In the app's Pipeline Lab, on salt-and-pepper noise that drops the page to 17.11 dB, the median recovers 31.18 dB; a 3 by 3 mean gets 24.26 and a Gaussian 24.58. A median ignores outliers; averaging smears them. SAY: The Lab shows the same story in frequency: the DFT spectrum of the page, and a low-pass filter that removes exactly the high frequencies the text lives in. SAY: And Unit 3 is in production too: every stored preview is JPEG at quality 88, 8 by 8 DCT blocks. The animation keeps fewer and fewer DCT coefficients of a letter-edge block, in zig-zag order, and reports PSNR live. In the app at quality 10 the sample scores 30.13 dB, at 50 it is 37.74, at 90 it is 45.28. On the synthetic benchmark, quality 10 keeps only 4.7 percent of the coefficients and the OCR error is still 0.35 percent: text survives aggressive DCT compression. BACK-POCKET: Why not filter in the frequency domain in the app? For a 3 by 3 or 5 by 5 kernel, direct convolution is cheaper than an FFT round trip; the convolution theorem says the result is the same. BACK-POCKET: Energy compaction: for smooth blocks the DCT puts most energy into a few low-frequency coefficients; text has real high-frequency energy, so very aggressive quantisation causes ringing; zonal coding breaks down below 3 by 3 coefficients (CER 0.75 percent at 3 by 3, 4.1 percent at 2 by 2). BACK-POCKET: The two PSNR sets are different measurements: the app numbers are one sample certificate on the phone, the benchmark numbers are six synthetic pages on a desktop.

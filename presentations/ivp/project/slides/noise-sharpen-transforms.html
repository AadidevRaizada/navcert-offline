TIME: 45 seconds. Unit 2 neighbourhood processing, Unit 3 transforms. SAY: Here is a paradox: to sharpen, we blur. Subtract a Gaussian-blurred copy from the image and what is left is the edge detail. Add a fraction of it back, 32 percent in Auto, 50 percent in Clean. The top curve is that equation on a single edge. SAY: Before that, a 3 by 3 median removes the speckle that CLAHE lifted. A median ignores outliers; a Gaussian would smear them. SAY: The Lab shows the same story in frequency: the DFT spectrum of the page, and a low-pass filter that removes exactly the high frequencies the text lives in. SAY: And Unit 3 is in production too: every stored preview is JPEG at quality 88, 8 by 8 DCT blocks. The bottom animation keeps fewer and fewer DCT coefficients of a letter-edge block, in zig-zag order, and reports PSNR live. Text is the hard case: strokes need high frequencies. BACK-POCKET: Why not filter in the frequency domain in the app? For a 3 by 3 or 5 by 5 kernel, direct convolution is cheaper than an FFT round trip; the convolution theorem says the result is the same. BACK-POCKET: Energy compaction: for smooth blocks the DCT puts most energy into a few low-frequency coefficients; text has real high-frequency energy, so aggressive quantisation causes ringing around characters. BACK-POCKET: Median versus Gaussian for salt-and-pepper noise: median, because outliers never get output while fewer than half the window is corrupted.
